Science is easier to talk about when people can play with it. These web applications translate results from my research into short interactive experiences: for poster sessions and lectures, for students, and for anyone who wonders why some trees survive a drought and others do not.

    <h2 id="app-death-spiral" class="app__title">Escape the Death Spiral</h2>
    <p>Keep a European beech or a Norway spruce alive through the real droughts of 2015, 2018–2020 and 2022, and the hotter years that follow. Tap clouds to make it rain into the soil, squash bark beetles before they reach the crown, and watch drought memory build up year after year.</p>
    <p>When your tree dies, the game tells you why: the <strong>predisposing</strong> dry years, the <strong>inciting</strong> drought and the <strong>contributing</strong> pests. This is Manion’s decline spiral, the same logic behind the drought-mortality framework I developed for the forest model ForClim.</p>
